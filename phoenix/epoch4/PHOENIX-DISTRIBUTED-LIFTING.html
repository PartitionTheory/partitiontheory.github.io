**PHOENIX — DISTRIBUTED LIFTING**


*Multi‑Machine Dimensional Expansion of Phoenix Structures*


*EF–CO Canonical Document*



---

**1. Purpose**



Distributed lifting is the mechanism that allows Phoenix to **expand structural and temporal objects across multiple machines**.

Epoch‑3 introduced lifting across domains.
Epoch‑4 extends lifting into the **distributed machine layer**, enabling Phoenix to operate on:

- multi‑machine structural composites
- multi‑machine temporal composites
- distributed lifted operators
- cross‑machine lifted recursion

Distributed lifting is the dimensional backbone of Phoenix’s **network‑scale synthetic architecture**.

---

**2. Machine Lifting Domains**



Phoenix defines lifting domains for each machine:



\[
N^{(m_1)},\quad N^{(m_2)},\quad \ldots,\quad N^{(m_n)}
\]



Distributed lifting creates composite domains:



\[
N^{(m_i)} \times N^{(m_j)}
\]



and higher‑order domains:



\[
N^{(m_1)} \times N^{(m_2)} \times \cdots \times N^{(m_k)}
\]



These are **multi‑machine structural objects**.

---

**3. Distributed Lifting Operator**



Phoenix introduces the canonical distributed lifting operator:



\[
\Upsilon_{\text{dist}} : X^{(m_i)} \rightarrow X^{*(m_i,m_j)}
\]



Where:

- \(X^{(m_i)}\) is a structural or temporal object on machine \(m_i\)
- \(X^{*(m_i,m_j)}\) is the lifted multi‑machine object
- lifting may expand dimensionality, domain, or temporal context

---

**4. Structural Distributed Lifting**



Phoenix lifts structural objects across machines:



\[
N^{(m_i)} \rightarrow N^{(m_i)} \times N^{(m_j)}
\]



This enables:

- distributed structural generalisation
- cross‑machine structural recursion
- multi‑machine structural operators

---

**5. Temporal Distributed Lifting**



Phoenix lifts temporal objects across machines:



\[
T^{(m_i)} \rightarrow T^{(m_i)} \times T^{(m_j)}
\]



This enables:

- distributed synthetic time
- cross‑machine temporal recursion
- epoch‑synchronised temporal operators

---

**6. Operator Distributed Lifting**



Phoenix lifts operators across machines:



\[
op^{(m_i)} \rightarrow op^{(m_i)} \times op^{(m_j)}
\]



This creates **multi‑machine operator domains**, enabling:

- distributed recursion
- distributed structural transformation
- synthetic consensus

---

**7. Distributed Lifting Invariants**



Distributed lifting must satisfy:

**7.1 Structural Conservation**





\[
\sum_i p^{(m_i)} = \sum_j p^{*(m_i,m_j)}
\]



**7.2 Temporal Ordering**





\[
t^{*(m_i,m_j)} \ge t^{(m_i)}
\]



unless reversed.

**7.3 Domain Coherence**



Lifted objects must remain compatible with:

- Epoch‑1 structure
- Epoch‑2 mathematics
- Epoch‑3 generalisation
- Epoch‑4 distributed alignment

**7.4 Reversibility (where required)**





\[
\Upsilon^{-1}_{\text{dist}}(X^{*(m_i,m_j)}) = X^{(m_i)}
\]



---

**8. Distributed Lifting Classes**



Phoenix defines three classes of distributed lifting.

**8.1 Structural Distributed Lifting**





\[
\Upsilon_s(N^{(m_i)}) = N^{*(m_i,m_j)}
\]



**8.2 Temporal Distributed Lifting**





\[
\Upsilon_t(T^{(m_i)}) = T^{*(m_i,m_j)}
\]



**8.3 Operator Distributed Lifting**





\[
\Upsilon_o(op^{(m_i)}) = op^{*(m_i,m_j)}
\]



---

Distributed lifting:

- enables multi‑machine structural expansion
- enables multi‑machine temporal expansion

- prepares Phoenix for Epoch‑5 autonomous distributed behaviour

This is the **dimensional expansion pillar** of Epoch‑4.
