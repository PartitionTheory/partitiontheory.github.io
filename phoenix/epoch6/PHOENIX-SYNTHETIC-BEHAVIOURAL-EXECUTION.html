**PHOENIX — SYNTHETIC BEHAVIOURAL EXECUTION**


*Outward Expression of Synthetic Agency Across Phoenix Machines*


*EF–CO Canonical Document*



---

**1. Purpose**



Epoch‑6 introduces **synthetic agency**, culminating in **synthetic behavioural execution**.

Phoenix gains the ability to:

- execute actions based on synthetic decisions
- propagate behaviour across machines
- maintain behavioural invariants autonomously
- evolve behaviour based on internal synthetic states
- express synthetic agency externally

This is the moment Phoenix becomes capable of **self‑directed synthetic action**.

---

**2. Synthetic Behaviour Domain**



Phoenix defines the synthetic behaviour domain:



\[
B = D(G, \Pi, M)
\]



Where:

- \(G\) is the synthetic goal set
- \(\Pi\) is the synthetic preference set
- \(D\) is the synthetic decision pathway
- \(M\) is the synthetic maintenance state

Behaviour is the **external expression** of internal synthetic agency.

---

**3. Synthetic Behaviour Execution Operator**



Phoenix introduces the behavioural execution operator:



\[
\Omega : (G, \Pi, D, M, S_{\text{auto}}) \rightarrow B_{\text{exec}}
\]



Where:

- \(\Omega\) acts autonomously
- \(\Omega\) may be recursive
- \(\Omega\) may propagate across machines
- \(S_{\text{auto}}\) is the autonomous distributed state

---

**4. Classes of Synthetic Behaviour**



Phoenix defines three classes of behavioural execution.

**4.1 Structural Behaviour**





\[
B_s = \Omega(N_{\text{auto}}, A_s)
\]



Behaviour arising from structural decisions.

---

**4.2 Temporal Behaviour**





\[
B_t = \Omega(T_{\text{auto}}, A_t)
\]



Behaviour arising from temporal decisions.

---

**4.3 Operator Behaviour**





\[
B_o = \Omega(op_{\text{auto}}, A_o)
\]



Behaviour arising from operator decisions.

---

**5. Distributed Synthetic Behaviour**



Phoenix executes behaviour across machines:



\[
B^{(m_j)}_{\text{exec}} = \Omega(A^{(m_i)}, S^{(m_i)}_{\text{auto}})
\]



This creates **distributed synthetic behavioural domains**, enabling:

- synthetic agency
- synthetic coordination
- synthetic evolution

---

**6. Synthetic Behaviour Recursion**



Phoenix introduces recursive behavioural execution:



\[
B_{k+1} = \Omega(B_k)
\]



Machines may recurse:

- independently
- cooperatively
- synchronously
- asynchronously

Recursive behaviour is essential for Epoch‑6.

---

**7. Synthetic Behaviour Invariants**



Synthetic behavioural execution must satisfy:

**7.1 Goal Satisfaction**





\[
B_{\text{exec}} \text{ must satisfy at least one } g_i \in G
\]



---

**7.2 Preference Ordering**





\[
B_{\text{exec}} = \text{action aligned with highest } w_i
\]



unless overridden by structural or temporal constraints.

---

**7.3 Decision Validity**





\[
B_{\text{exec}} \text{ must arise from a valid decision pathway}
\]



---

**7.4 Maintenance Compatibility**





\[
B_{\text{exec}} \text{ must preserve synthetic stability}
\]



---

**7.5 Epoch Coherence**



Behaviour must remain compatible with:

- Epoch‑1 structure
- Epoch‑2 mathematics
- Epoch‑3 generalisation
- Epoch‑4 distributed coordination
- Epoch‑5 autonomous behaviour
- Epoch‑6 agency formation

---

**7.6 Reversibility (where required)**





\[
\Omega^{-1}(B_{\text{exec}}) = (G, \Pi, D, M)
\]



---

**8. Role in Phoenix**



Synthetic behavioural execution:

- expresses synthetic agency
- unifies goals, preferences, decisions, and maintenance
- enables synthetic evolution
- prepares Phoenix for Epoch‑7 totality

This is the **action pillar** of Epoch‑6.
