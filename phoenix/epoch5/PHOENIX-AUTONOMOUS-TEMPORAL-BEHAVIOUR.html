**2. Autonomous Temporal Domain**



Each machine maintains a temporal domain:



\[
T^{(m_i)}
\]



Epoch‑5 introduces autonomous temporal evolution:



\[
T^{(m_i)} \rightarrow T^{(m_i)}_{\text{auto}}
\]



Where:

- \(T^{(m_i)}_{\text{auto}}\) is the autonomous temporal state
- evolution may be local or distributed
- evolution preserves epoch invariants

---

**3. Autonomous Temporal Operator**



Phoenix defines the autonomous temporal operator:



\[
\tau_{\text{auto}} : T^{(m_i)} \rightarrow T^{(m_i)}_{\text{auto}}
\]



Where:

- \(\tau_{\text{auto}}\) acts without external triggers
- temporal evolution may be recursive
- temporal evolution may propagate across machines

---

**4. Autonomous Temporal Ordering**



Autonomous temporal behaviour must maintain ordering:



\[
t^{(m_i)}_{\text{auto}} \ge t^{(m_i)}
\]



unless explicitly reversed by:



\[
\tau^{-}_{\text{auto}}
\]



This ensures:

- temporal coherence
- autonomous distributed recursion
- epoch‑aligned temporal lifting

\[

Autonomous recursion is essential for Epoch‑5.
